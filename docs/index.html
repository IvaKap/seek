You pick the copy.

Seek is an unofficial Soulseek client for macOS. When several people have the same record, it lays them side by side — how many tracks, what format, who has a slot free, how they've treated you before — and then gets out of the way.

Why another client

It shows you the data instead of deciding for you.

No silent substitutions

Click Get on a nine-track copy and you get that nine-track copy. If a fuller one exists elsewhere, Seek shows you — it never quietly swaps your choice for one it prefers.

It admits what it can't know

Where nobody has the whole album, Seek says so and lists what each person actually has, file by file. It won't claim one person's "track 4" is the same recording as another's, because that guess was measured against real rips and was wrong more often than right.

Reliability means your history

"9 of 19 with you" comes from your own transfer log. Soulseek exposes nothing about how a stranger treats anyone else, so nothing here pretends to a reputation score it cannot see.

Getting it running

macOS will stop you the first time. Here's the way past.

Seek isn't signed with a paid Apple certificate, so macOS treats it as unverified. Nothing is wrong with the download — this is what every unsigned app gets.

What you'll see

"Apple could not verify “Seek” is free of malware."

  1. Move Seek to Applications, then try to open it. Dismiss the warning.
  2. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security.
  3. Scroll down. There's a line saying Seek was blocked — click Open Anyway.
  4. Confirm. macOS remembers, and won't ask again.

Prefer one command instead? xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Seek.app

Or build it yourself

An app you build on your own Mac isn't quarantined and opens normally. You'll need Rust, Node 20+ and Python 3.11+. 

git clone --recurse-submodules <repo>
cd seek && ./release.sh

Optional

Bring your own keys.

Searching and downloading need nothing. These extras stay switched off until you add a free key in Settings → External lookups, and the keys never leave your Mac except to reach the service they belong to.
